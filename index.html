A crop model is an argument about what limits a plant. SIMPLE makes that argument in one line of arithmetic, repeated once a day, from sowing to maturity.

Biomass_rate = Radiation × fSolar × RUE × f(CO2) × f(Temp) × min[ f(Heat), f(Water) ]

Zhao et al. (2019), Equation 3. Everything this app does is an exploration of that product.

Read it from the left. Radiation is what the sky offers. fSolar is the share of it the canopy actually catches. RUE is how efficiently the crop turns caught light into dry matter. The remaining terms are all fractions between 0 and 1 — they can only take away.

Note the min[ ]. Heat and drought do not stack. On any given day the crop is held back by whichever is worse, following Liebig's law of the minimum. That single choice explains a great deal of what you will see in the sandbox.

Three things decide a yield

The controls in this app are grouped the way agronomists actually think about a field — the G × E × M framing:

  • G — the seed. What the breeder fixed before the season started: radiation use efficiency, harvest index, how fast the canopy closes, how long it takes to mature, which temperatures the crop can tolerate.
  • E — the sky and the ground. What the season delivers and what the field holds: temperature, rainfall, radiation, CO2, and the soil's capacity to store water between showers.
  • M — the farmer. What is actually decided on the ground: sowing date, plant density, whether to irrigate.

A parameter that dominates in one combination barely matters in another. The Analyse section ranks them for whatever situation you have set up, which is more useful than any fixed list.

How the season is timed

SIMPLE has no calendar. It has a thermometer. Each day it banks the mean temperature above a base threshold, and the crop matures when the bank reaches Tsum:

ΔTT = max(0, T̄ − Tbase)    TTi+1 = TTi + ΔTT    maturity when TT ≥ Tsum

This is why warming is not simply good or bad. A warmer season fills the thermal bank faster, so the crop matures sooner — fewer days of light captured, less biomass — even before any heat damage occurs. Push the temperature slider and watch the season length fall before the heat-stress counter ever moves.

How the canopy opens and closes

fSolar is two logistic curves meeting at a minimum: one for expansion, one for senescence.

fSolar = min[  fmax / (1 + e−0.01(TT − I50A)),   fmax / (1 + e 0.01(TT − (Tsum − I50B)))  ]

I50A sets how quickly the canopy builds; I50B sets how long it stays up. And I50B is not constant — every day of heat or drought adds to it, which pulls senescence forward:

I50Bi+1 = I50Bi + I50maxH(1 − f(Heat)) + I50maxW(1 − f(Water))

This is the model's memory. A heat wave in week four is still costing you light in week ten, because the canopy aged.

Why fSolar and not leaf area SIMPLE deliberately tracks intercepted light rather than leaf area index, which removes a whole layer of parameters. The 3D plant in the Grow section runs that choice backwards — inverting Beer-Lambert, LAI = −ln(1 − fSolar) / k — to recover a leaf area it can actually draw. The plant is a picture of the model's state, not a separate simulation.

Where the water comes from

Rain that falls is not rain that is used. A curve-number step removes runoff, a drainage step removes what the soil cannot hold, and what remains is available for transpiration. The shortfall between what the crop wanted and what it got is the ARID index, and f(Water) follows from it:

ARID = 1 − (Transpiration / ETo)    f(Water) = 1 − Swater × ARID

Swater is a species trait. Maize sits at 1.2, wheat at 0.4 — the same dry spell costs them very different amounts.

The gap worth measuring

Running the same crop twice — once with water limitation and once without — gives two numbers that frame every yield conversation:

  • Yp, potential yield. Water is never limiting. Set by radiation, temperature, CO2 and genetics alone.
  • Yw, water-limited yield. The crop lives on rainfall and whatever the soil stored.
  • The yield gap is the difference — and it tells you whether the constraint in front of you is water, or something irrigation cannot fix.
A caution about the numbers The weather here is generated from smooth seasonal functions, not measured records, and some cultivar Tsum values have been recalibrated to suit those synthetic climates. Treat every absolute yield as illustrative. The relationships — the direction and shape of each response — are what this app is for.
